# Legal, tax and compliance framework for a web-development "popoldanski s.p." in Slovenia (status: 31 October 2026)

Research date: 31 October 2026. This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Every figure below carries the date of its source; anything only confirmed for 2025 or earlier is labelled. Two moving parts dominate 2026: (1) **ZPZR** (normiranci reform, in force for 2026) and its amendment **ZPZR-A** (in force 7 March 2026); (2) **ZIURS** (Zakon o interventnih ukrepih za razvoj Slovenije), adopted 11 May 2026 but blocked pending a **referendum on 6 December 2026**. If ZIURS survives the referendum, its normiranci and popoldanski provisions would apply retroactively from 1 January 2026. Confirm the outcome with a računovodja before filing the 2026 return.

## 1. Popoldanski s.p. basics in 2026: conditions, registration, SKD 2025 codes, employer notification and ZDR-1 prepoved konkurence

### Takeaway
To open a popoldanski s.p. you must already be employed full-time (40 h/week; a 36-hour full-time contract may also qualify). Registration through SPOT is free and online. The SKD 2025 code for web and software development is **K62.100 Računalniško programiranje**, which explicitly covers websites. The law does not oblige you to tell your employer, but under **ZDR-1 Article 39** you need the employer's **written consent** if the s.p. work falls within the activity your employer actually performs and is, or could be, competition. Check this before signing the first client.

### Cited Findings
- **Core condition:** you must be employed full-time (polni delovni čas, 40 hours/week). The form of the employment contract does not matter: fixed-term or open-ended both qualify. Source: data.si guide (2026) — [data.si: Odprtje popoldanskega s.p.](https://data.si/blog/odprtje-popoldanskega-s-p-3/). Data d.o.o. (14 Apr 2026) also describes the flat contributions as owed by "people employed full-time (40 hours/week) who register a popoldanski s.p." — [Data d.o.o.](https://data.si/blog/novi-prispevki-za-popoldanski-s-p/)
- **Who cannot open one:** according to data.si, students, pensioners and people employed part-time cannot open a *popoldanski* s.p. — [data.si: popoldanski s.p.](https://data.si/popoldanski-s-p/). **Conflict:** another data.si article says a 36-hour week that counts as full-time under ZDR-1 can also qualify — [data.si: popoldanski s.p. in polni delovni čas](https://data.si/blog/popoldanski-sp-polni-delovni-cas/). Confirm with ZZZS or SPOT if your contract is under 40 hours.
- **Registration is free.** You can file online on the SPOT portal (this needs a qualified digital certificate) or in person at a SPOT Registracija point: AJPES branches, upravne enote, GZS, OZS and other chambers. AJPES enters the s.p. into the Poslovni register Slovenije (PRS). Registration with FURS and with compulsory social insurance follows from the same filing — [SPOT: Ustanovitev s.p.](https://spot.gov.si/sl/teme/ustanovitev-s-p/). An s.p. cannot be registered at a notary, only at a SPOT point or through the portal — [SPOT: seznam storitev točk SPOT in notarjev](https://spot.gov.si/sl/portal-in-tocke-spot/seznam-storitev-tock-spot-in-notarjev/)
- **SKD 2025 is in use.** The new Standardna klasifikacija dejavnosti (SKD 2025) was announced for 1 January 2025 — [SPOT napoved, 11 Sep 2024](https://spot.gov.si/sl/portal-in-tocke-spot/novice/2024-09-11-085000-napoved-s-1-1-2025-se-uvaja-nova-razlicica-standardne-klasifikacije-dejavnosti-skd-2025). Government notice of its start in January 2025: [gov.si, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-01-16-zacela-se-je-uporaba-nove-standardne-klasifikacije-dejavnosti-skd-2025/)
- **AJPES conversion:** AJPES converted 1:1 and N:1 code matches automatically. Where one old code split into several new ones (1:M), businesses had to enter the correct code manually on the AJPES portal — [data.si: SKD 2025](https://data.si/blog/skd-2025-in-dolocitev-dejavnosti/)
- **K62.100 Računalniško programiranje (SKD 2025) includes:**
  - writing, changing, adapting and testing software;
  - designing and maintaining the structure and content of software and applications, including updates and bug fixes;
  - the listed examples include **websites**, databases, business applications and machine learning/AI;
  - adapting existing applications for a client's environment.

  It **excludes** design of combined hardware/software/communications systems (62.200), data processing (63.100) and software publishing (58.2). SPOT lists **no prescribed entry conditions** (licence or qualification) for this subclass. General duties still apply: copyright, personal data, use of Slovene and so on — [SPOT: K62.100](https://spot.gov.si/sl/dejavnosti-in-poklici/dejavnosti/racunalnisko-programiranje-62-100)
- **ZDR-1 Article 39(1) (prepoved konkurence):** "Med trajanjem delovnega razmerja delavec ne sme brez pisnega soglasja delodajalca za svoj ali tuj račun opravljati del ali sklepati poslov, ki sodijo v dejavnost, ki jo dejansko opravlja delodajalec in pomenijo ali bi lahko pomenili za delodajalca konkurenco." In English: during employment, the employee may not, without the employer's written consent, work or conclude deals for their own or another's account if these fall within the activity the employer actually performs and are, or could be, competition for the employer. Version published 19 Sep 2025, in force 20 Sep 2025 — [racunovodstvo.net: ZDR-1 39. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zdr/39-clen)
- **ZDR-1 Article 39(2):** the employer may claim damages within **3 months** of learning of the work or deal, or within **3 years** of the work being finished or the deal concluded. The article sets **no deadline** for the employer to answer a consent request — [racunovodstvo.net: ZDR-1 39. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zdr/39-clen)
- **Case law (as surfaced in search; I did not open the full decisions):**
  - preparatory acts can fall under Article 39, but merely showing an intent to compete *after* the employment ends does not;
  - unpaid minor favours were held not to be gainful activity (VDSS Pdp 592/2014);
  - a breach can support dismissal.

  Sources: [TFL](https://tax-fin-lex.si/Dokument/Podrobnosti/2306323a-2380-4ff9-ab43-95c8e8036fa4); [TFL](https://tax-fin-lex.si/Dokument/Podrobnosti/95b95614-31f4-46f5-9774-0acf25e4c34c)
- **Public servants:** a new public-servants act, ZJU-1, exists (consolidated text dated 1 Jan 2026) and has a separate regime. Its Article 100 text was not retrieved — [TFL: ZJU-1 čistopis 1.1.2026](https://tax-fin-lex.si/Dokument/Podrobnosti/84516a91-245e-4a2f-88ad-eb5f670c5dc3)

### Inferences
- **Code mapping:** the old SKD 2008 code **J62.010** "Računalniško programiranje" maps to SKD 2025 **K62.100** (NACE 62.01 → 62.10). Bizi.si listings show s.p.-s whose codes were changed in January 2025 "zaradi spremembe SKD". Pick K62.100 as the main activity.
- **Optional extra codes:**
  - 62.200 if you sell IT consulting or system design;
  - the 63.1x hosting/data-processing class if you sell hosting as a separate service;
  - a 74.1x design code if you sell pure graphic design.

  Exact SKD 2025 names should be checked on the AJPES/SURS lists.
- **When employer consent is needed:** the test is the employer's **actual** activity, not its registered codes.
  - If your employer is a web agency, software house or digital-marketing firm that builds sites for clients, you need **written** consent before starting.
  - If your employer is, for example, a bank or manufacturer with an internal IT department, an s.p. building SME websites is unlikely to be "konkurenca", but it is a grey zone.
  - Either way, getting written consent removes the dismissal and damages risk.
- **Check your employment contract** for a konkurenčna klavzula (post-employment non-compete, ZDR-1 Art. 40 ff.; article numbers from memory, not verified here) and for IP/moonlighting clauses. Never use the employer's equipment, code or working time for the s.p.
- **Notifying the employer:** I found no general statutory duty to inform an employer that you have opened an s.p. The duty arises only through Article 39, through contract terms, or, for public servants, through ZJU-1. The s.p. is publicly visible in AJPES/PRS.
- **Hours:** I found no statutory cap on hours worked in the s.p. The binding constraint is keeping the full-time employment. If that employment ends, the s.p. would presumably switch to full s.p. contributions (minimum about €649/month in 2026, see Q2); confirm with ZZZS/FURS.

### Gaps
- No official SPOT, ZZZS or FURS page on popoldanski s.p. conditions was retrieved. The 36- vs 40-hour question rests on data.si.
- The exact official SKD 2008→2025 conversion for 62.010 and for hosting codes (63.1x) was not confirmed from the Klasje/SURS tables.
- The text of ZJU-1 Article 100 (additional work by public servants) was not retrieved.
- No official guidance was found on whether opening an s.p. in the same field is itself a breach before any deals are done. Court practice suggests preparatory acts *can* count.

## 2. Contributions and taxes in 2026: flat contributions, normirani vs dejanski stroški, normiranec thresholds, tax rates, akontacija, worked examples and announced changes

### Takeaway
A popoldanski s.p. pays **flat monthly contributions**:
- **€110.11** (January–March 2026): PIZ 49.15 + ZZ 54.53 + DO 6.43;
- **€113.01** from **April 2026** (PIZ 52.05);
- about **€1,347** for the whole of 2026, owed every month the s.p. is registered.

**Normiranci rules for 2026 (ZPZR + ZPZR-A):**
- A popoldanski s.p. may use normirani odhodki if its **two-year average revenue ≤ €50,000**.
- Recognised costs are tiered: **80% up to €12,500, 40% from €12,500 to €30,000, 0% above** (maximum €17,000).
- The tax is **final**: **20% on a tax base up to €33,000**, then €6,600 + 35% on the excess.

At €10k / €25k / €50k revenue the income tax is €400 / €2,000 / €6,600 (4% / 8% / 13.2% of revenue). With contributions, total levies are about €1.75k / €3.35k / €7.95k. **ZIURS** would raise the popoldanski limit to €70,000 and recognise 80% of revenue up to €50,000. It is pending the 6 Dec 2026 referendum.

### Cited Findings
**Flat contributions (popoldanski s.p.)**
- **Jan–Mar 2026:** PIZ €49.15 + ZZ (zdravstveno zavarovanje) €54.53 + DO (dolgotrajna oskrba) €6.43 = **€110.11/month**. No separate OZP line is listed for popoldanski s.p. Source: OOZ Cerknica, 10 Feb 2026 — [OOZ Cerknica](https://www.oozc.si/obvestila/prispevki-za-polni-s-p-popoldanski-s-p-2026-in-placevanje-prispevkov/)
- **From 1 April 2026:** PIZ €52.05 + ZZ €54.53 + DO €6.43 = **€113.01/month**. Data d.o.o., 14 Apr 2026 — [Data d.o.o.](https://data.si/blog/novi-prispevki-za-popoldanski-s-p/)
- **How the PIZ amount is set:** by the "Sklep o določitvi prispevkov za posebne primere zavarovanja", adopted each March and applied from April to the following March.
  - The popoldanski PIZ contribution covers **invalidnost, telesna okvara and smrt** (invalidity, bodily impairment and death).
  - If the activity lasts **fewer than 15 days in a month, half** the PIZ contribution is due.

  Source: [OOZ Cerknica, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.oozc.si/obvestila/prispevki-za-polni-s-p-popoldanski-s-p-2026-in-placevanje-prispevkov/)
- **Payment:**
  - FURS prepares the "Obračun prispevkov" (PODO_OPSVD) in eDavki by the **10th** of each month.
  - The s.p. pays by the **20th** of the month for the previous month, together with the akontacija dohodnine, using model 19 and the tax number with a suffix.
  - PIZ goes to SI56 0110 0888 2000 003 with suffix -44008.
  - ZZ+DO go to SI56 0110 0888 3000 073 with suffix -45004.
  - Late payment accrues interest.

  Source: [OOZ Cerknica](https://www.oozc.si/obvestila/prispevki-za-polni-s-p-popoldanski-s-p-2026-in-placevanje-prispevkov/)
- **Full-time s.p. for comparison (2026):**
  - minimum contributions €648.85/month (2025: €614.83, which still applied to January 2026);
  - minimum base is 60% of the 2025 average gross wage (€2,536.03), i.e. €1,521.62;
  - maximum contributions €3,605.38.

  Source: [OOZ Cerknica, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.oozc.si/obvestila/prispevki-za-polni-s-p-popoldanski-s-p-2026-in-placevanje-prispevkov/). The €1,521.62 minimum base applies from 1 Mar 2026 to 28 Feb 2027 — [spnomad.si, updated 19 Oct 2026](https://www.spnomad.si/objave/interventni-zakon-2026-spremembe-za-sp)

**Normiranci 2026 — legal basis**
- **ZPZR:** *Zakon o pravici do zimskega regresa ter prenovi ugotavljanja davčne osnove z upoštevanjem normiranih odhodkov*, Uradni list RS, št. 91/25, published 19 Nov 2025, applying from 1 Jan 2026 — [FURS/gov.si, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-24-novosti-pri-ugotavljanju-davcne-osnove-z-upostevanjem-normiranih-odhodkov-v-letu-2026/); [minimax.si, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.minimax.si/sl-si/novosti-pri-normirancih-v-letu-2026)
- **ZPZR-A:** adopted 26 Feb 2026, in force **7 March 2026**, applying to tax year 2026. Entry is now tested on the **average revenue of the two preceding tax years**. A taxpayer may enter normiranje if that average does not exceed:
  - **€120,000** if self-employed-insured for ≥75% of the minimum annual full-time hours in both years;
  - **€50,000** if not so insured in either year (this is the popoldanski case);
  - **€85,000** in mixed cases.

  Partial years are prorated by days. Example: 90 days in each of 2024 and 2025 → 180/731 × €50,000 ≈ €12,311.90. The **five-year re-entry ban was abolished**. The insurance test follows Constitutional Court decision U-I-43/24-17 (13 Nov 2025). A transitional opt-in (form NF-LD) was open until 31 March 2026 — [FURS/gov.si, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.gov.si/novice/2026-03-12-omilitev-pogojev-za-vstop-v-sistem-normiranih-odhodkov/)
- **Exit rule:** a taxpayer must switch to actual revenues and expenses if the same two-year average exceeds €120,000 / €50,000 / €85,000 — [PISRS: ZPZR consolidated text incl. ZPZR-A, Art. 6(3), 6(13)](https://pisrs.si/api/datoteke/integracije/486867865). A year without activity counts as **zero revenue**. Example: an s.p. started 1 June 2025 with €110,000 revenue averages €55,000, exceeds €50,000 and must exit for 2026 — [FURS/gov.si, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-24-novosti-pri-ugotavljanju-davcne-osnove-z-upostevanjem-normiranih-odhodkov-v-letu-2026/)

**Normiranci 2026 — recognised costs and tax rates**
- **Recognised normirani odhodki (ZDoh-2 Art. 59, version applicable from 1 Jan 2025):**
  - Taxpayers *not* full-time self-employed-insured (popoldanski s.p.): **80% up to €12,500; 40% from €12,500 to €30,000; 0% above €30,000.**
  - Taxpayers insured full-time for at least nine months: 80% up to €60,000, 0% above.

  Source: [racunovodstvo.net: ZDoh-2 59. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zdoh/59-clen). This gives a maximum of €17,000 for popoldanski s.p. and €48,000 for full s.p. — [minimax.si, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.minimax.si/sl-si/novosti-pri-normirancih-v-letu-2026)
- **Tax rates (ZPZR Art. 7):**
  - The tax is **final** (dokončen davek, Art. 7(3)).
  - Not full-time insured: **20% on a tax base up to €33,000; above that €6,600 + 35% of the excess over €33,000.**
  - Full-time insured: 20% up to €72,000; above that €14,400 + 35%.

  Source: [PISRS: ZPZR consolidated](https://pisrs.si/api/datoteke/integracije/486867865). The rates apply to the **tax base** (revenue minus normirani odhodki), not to revenue — [FURS/gov.si, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-24-novosti-pri-ugotavljanju-davcne-osnove-z-upostevanjem-normiranih-odhodkov-v-letu-2026/)
- **Official worked examples:**
  - Full s.p. with €130,000 revenue: normirani €48,000 → base €82,000 → tax €14,400 + €3,500 = **€17,900** — [FURS/gov.si, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-24-novosti-pri-ugotavljanju-davcne-osnove-z-upostevanjem-normiranih-odhodkov-v-letu-2026/)
  - Popoldanski s.p. with €60,000 revenue: costs €17,000 → €33,000 at 20% (€6,600) + €10,000 at 35% (€3,500) = **€10,100** — [minimax.si, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.minimax.si/sl-si/novosti-pri-normirancih-v-letu-2026)
- **Normiranec income is outside the annual tax base.** FURS describes it as "dohodek iz dejavnosti, ki se ne všteva v letno davčno osnovo (normiranci)" — [SPOT/FURS, 22 Dec 2025](https://spot.gov.si/sl/portal-in-tocke-spot/novice/2025-12-22-102600-furs-obvesca-nove-stopnje-dohodnine-od-dohodka-iz-dejavnosti-ki-se-ne-vsteva-v-letno-davcno-osnovo-normiranci)

**Akontacija dohodnine**
- The SPOT form computes akontacija at **20%** and always applies **80%** normirani odhodki, whatever the insurance status. It does **not** use the new ZPZR scale — [SPOT/FURS, 22 Dec 2025](https://spot.gov.si/sl/portal-in-tocke-spot/novice/2025-12-22-102600-furs-obvesca-nove-stopnje-dohodnine-od-dohodka-iz-dejavnosti-ki-se-ne-vsteva-v-letno-davcno-osnovo-normiranci)
- Akontacija is due by the **20th of the month** (unified deadline since 2023) — [Siol, 2023](https://siol.net/novice/slovenija/podjetniki-pozor-po-novem-je-rok-za-placilo-akontacije-dohodnine-20-v-mesecu-598346). Contributions are paid together with it — [OOZ Cerknica](https://www.oozc.si/obvestila/prispevki-za-polni-s-p-popoldanski-s-p-2026-in-placevanje-prispevkov/)

**Changes pending for 2026–2027: ZIURS**
- **FURS notice of 16 Oct 2026 (updated 21 Oct):**
  - ZIURS regulates normirani odhodki for 2026 and would apply from 1 Jan 2026 if it enters into force;
  - its fate "je odvisna od rezultata na naknadnem zakonodajnem referendumu" (depends on the result of the subsequent legislative referendum);
  - if ZIURS does not enter into force, ZPZR applies for 2026;

  Source: [FURS/gov.si, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.si/novice/2026-10-16-ugotavljanje-davcne-osnove-z-normiranimi-odhodki-v-letu-2026-v-povezavi-z-interventnim-zakonom-ziurs/)
- **ZIURS timeline (blog summary, updated 19 Oct 2026):**
  - adopted by the DZ on 11 May 2026 (47:35);

  - the unions filed on 9 Oct 2026 with more than 40,000 signatures;
  - **referendum on Sunday, 6 December 2026**;
  - the law is rejected only if a majority votes against *and* those votes are at least one-fifth of all voters.

  Source: [spnomad.si](https://www.spnomad.si/objave/interventni-zakon-2026-spremembe-za-sp)

  - for full normiranci: 80% up to €100k, 70% for €100–120k, 40% for €120–150k, exit above €150k;
  - a new "micro s.p." status with contributions based on 45% of the minimum wage;
  - rental income tax cut from 25% to 15%.

  Source: [spnomad.si](https://www.spnomad.si/objave/interventni-zakon-2026-spremembe-za-sp)

### Inferences
**Worked examples for 2026 (popoldanski s.p., normiranec, ZPZR rules; assumes ZIURS is rejected or changes nothing)**

Contributions 2026 = 3 × €110.11 + 9 × €113.01 = **€1,347.42**. Contributions are not separately deductible for normiranci; the normirani odhodki replace all real costs.

| Annual revenue | Normirani odhodki | Tax base | Tax (20%) | Tax % of revenue | Tax + contributions | Left before real costs* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| €10,000 | 80% × 10,000 = €8,000 | €2,000 | **€400** | 4.0% | €1,747 (17.5%) | ≈ €8,253 |
| €25,000 | 10,000 + 40% × 12,500 = €15,000 | €10,000 | **€2,000** | 8.0% | €3,347 (13.4%) | ≈ €21,653 |
| €50,000 | 10,000 + 40% × 17,500 = €17,000 | €33,000 | **€6,600** | 13.2% | €7,947 (15.9%) | ≈ €42,053 |

*Real costs still to be paid out of this: Vercel, Supabase and other tools, the accountant, insurance, and any non-deductible reverse-charge VAT (see Q3).

**Comparison with dejanski stroški (illustrative only).** Profit is added to the salary in the annual dohodnina, so it is taxed at the user's marginal rate (likely 26–39% for a full-time employee).
- €25,000 revenue − €2,000 real costs − €1,347 contributions = about €21,650 profit. At 26–33%, tax is about **€5,600–7,150**, against **€2,000** as a normiranec.
- At €50,000 the gap is larger: about €15,000–17,800 against €6,600.

Dejanski stroški only make sense with high real costs. They also need full bookkeeping (higher accountant fees), although they avoid the €50k two-year-average cap.

**If ZIURS survives the 6 Dec referendum (illustration; assumes the 20% rate):**
- €25,000 → normirani €20,000 → base €5,000 → about €1,000 tax;
- €50,000 → normirani €40,000 → base €10,000 → about €2,000 tax;
- contributions about €77/year lower (no DO).

The ZIURS rate scale for normiranci was not verified.

**Practical consequences**
- **Revenue counts by accounting rules** ("prihodki po računovodskih pravilih"). Invoices issued in late December count toward that year's average. Watch the two-year **€50,000** average for staying a normiranec and the **€60,000** VAT threshold (Q3).
- **Hidden effect of the 80% akontacija:** because the SPOT form assumes 80% normirani odhodki and a 20% rate, akontacija for a popoldanski s.p. above €12,500 revenue will be **too low**. Expect a top-up payment with the annual return.
- **Pension:** the popoldanski PIZ contribution only covers invalidity, bodily impairment and death. It most likely does **not** build pension years (pokojninska doba). Confirm with ZPIZ.

### Gaps
- The legal basis and calculation of the ZZ amount (€54.53/month) and the DO amount (€6.43) for popoldanski s.p. were not found in an official source; amounts come from OOZ and Data d.o.o.
- No 2027 contribution amounts exist yet. The PIZ amount resets each April by Sklep.
- The akontacija instalment rule (quarterly if annual akontacija ≤ €400, otherwise monthly) and first-year treatment are reported only by secondary sources. I could not open an official FURS page.
- The ZIURS law text was not retrieved. It is unclear whether ZIURS changes the 20%/35% scale or the €33,000 bracket for popoldanci.
- Statutory tax reliefs under dejanski stroški (e.g., investment relief) were not researched.

## 3. VAT: €60,000 threshold, exceeding it, EU business clients, EU consumers (OSS) and services bought from Vercel/Supabase/Stripe

### Takeaway
- **Registration threshold:** a Slovenian s.p. stays outside the VAT system while Slovenian turnover is **≤ €60,000** in the previous and current calendar year, with a buffer up to **€66,000** in the current year. These rules have applied since 1 Jan 2025.
- **Special VAT ID even if not registered:** a non-VAT-registered s.p. must still obtain an **identifikacijska številka za DDV** ("special" identification, ZDDV-1 Art. 79(1) points 4–5) when it:
  - **receives** B2B services from foreign suppliers for which it must self-assess VAT (Vercel Inc. – US, Supabase Pte. Ltd. – Singapore, foreign ad platforms); or
  - **supplies** services to EU business clients.
- **Cost of self-assessed VAT:** the s.p. then self-assesses 22% Slovenian VAT on those purchases **without a deduction right**.
- **EU consumers:** B2C electronically supplied services (e.g., automated hosting/SaaS) move to the consumer's country above an EU-wide **€10,000** threshold, reported through OSS.

### Cited Findings
- **ZDDV-1 Art. 94 (version published 10 Dec 2024, applying from 1 Jan 2025):**
  - exemption for a taxpayer whose Slovenian turnover did not exceed **€60,000** in the previous calendar year and does not exceed it in the current year;
  - a taxpayer exceeding €60,000 during the year keeps the exemption until turnover exceeds **€66,000**;
  - an exempt taxpayer **may not show VAT on invoices** and has **no right to deduct** input VAT (para. 8);
  - voluntary opt-in to VAT must be notified electronically in advance and binds the taxpayer for at least the current and following calendar year (para. 9).

  Source: [racunovodstvo.net: ZDDV-1 94. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zddv/94-clen)
- **Exceeding the threshold (secondary source):**
  - past €66,000, VAT applies from the supply that exceeds the limit;
  - between €60,000 and €66,000, the taxpayer registers for the following year.

  Source: [Taxenlight: VAT in Slovenia 2026](https://www.taxenlight.com/en/vat-in-slovenia/). Registration uses form DDV-P2, and some EU transactions require it earlier — [SPOT: Splošno o davkih](https://spot.gov.si/sl/teme/splosno-o-davkih)
- **ZDDV-1 Art. 79(1) (same version) requires FURS to identify for VAT:**
  - **point 4:** "vsakega davčnega zavezanca, ki v Sloveniji prejme storitve, za katere je dolžan plačati DDV" under Art. 76(1)(3) — every taxable person receiving services in Slovenia for which it must pay the VAT (reverse charge);
  - **point 5:** every taxable person seated in Slovenia who supplies services in another member state where the recipient is liable under Art. 196 of Directive 2006/112/EC;
  - **Art. 79(2):** the VAT ID is the tax number with the prefix "SI".

  Source: [racunovodstvo.net: ZDDV-1 79. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zddv/79-clen)
- **Timing of identification:** the implementing rules say a person likely to receive such reverse-charge services should obtain the VAT ID for the month in which it is probable they will receive or perform them. This comes from a search summary of the Pravilnik o izvajanju ZDDV-1 — [racunovodstvo.net: PZDDV](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/pzddv)
- **Cross-border reverse charge (EU guidance):** for services bought from abroad, the business recipient generally accounts for the VAT as if it were the supplier. Selling to EU business clients follows the same reverse-charge logic — [Your Europe: DDV pri čezmejnem poslovanju](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/vat-customs/cross-border/index_sl.htm)
- **Supplier entities:** Vercel's DPA names **Vercel Inc.** (Delaware, US) — [Vercel DPA](https://vercel.com/legal/dpa). Supabase's DPA names **Supabase Pte. Ltd.** (Singapore) — [Supabase DPA](https://supabase.com/legal/dpa). Both are non-EU suppliers.
- **OSS and the €10,000 threshold (FURS guide, 5th edition, January 2025):**
  - an EU-wide **€10,000** annual threshold applies to cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and **electronic services** plus intra-EU distance sales of goods;
  - it applies whether or not the business is VAT-identified in Slovenia;
  - once exceeded, the place of taxation moves to the consumer's member state;
  - OSS is an optional way to report and pay that foreign VAT;
  - other B2C services do not count toward the threshold.

  Source: [FURS: Posebne ureditve VEM (OSS)](https://www.fu.gov.si/fileadmin/Internet/Drugo/Posebna_podrocja/E_trgovanje/Posebne_ureditve_VEM-OSS.docx)
- **Electronically supplied services** under Annex II of the VAT Directive include "website supply, web-hosting, distance maintenance of programmes and equipment" — [EUR-Lex: Directive 2006/112/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj)
- **EU cross-border SME scheme:** since 1 Jan 2025, Directive (EU) 2020/285 allows small businesses to use VAT exemptions in other member states, subject to an EU-wide turnover cap of €100,000 — [EUR-Lex: Directive (EU) 2020/285](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2020/285/oj). ZDDV-1 Art. 94(2) covers foreign taxpayers who use that scheme in Slovenia — [racunovodstvo.net](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zddv/94-clen)
- **Standard rate:** the Slovenian standard VAT rate is 22% — [Taxenlight: VAT in Slovenia 2026](https://www.taxenlight.com/en/vat-in-slovenia/)

### Inferences
**Buying foreign B2B services (Vercel Pro, Supabase Pro, Stripe Billing, foreign ad platforms, LLM APIs)**
- From the first such purchase, the s.p. should hold an SI VAT ID under Art. 79(1)(4), give it to the suppliers, and self-assess **22% VAT** on those invoices in the DDV-O return.
- Because the s.p. is not fully VAT-registered, that VAT **cannot be deducted**. In practice this adds about 22% to SaaS and infrastructure costs.
- Missing this is a common error. The accountant should set it up at registration.

**Selling to EU business clients**
- For web development (the general B2B rule), the place of supply is the client's country. The invoice carries no Slovenian VAT, includes a reverse-charge note and shows both VAT IDs.
- Check the client's VAT number in VIES before invoicing.
- The s.p. needs the SI VAT ID (Art. 79(1)(5)) and must file the recapitulative statement (rekapitulacijsko poročilo). The latter is not verified in sources here.

**Selling to EU consumers**
- **Custom web design/development delivered with human effort:** most likely the general B2C rule applies, with the place of supply in Slovenia, so it is covered by the Slovenian small-business exemption. Under Implementing Regulation 282/2011, electronically supplied services are those that are essentially automated; to be confirmed.
- **Automated hosting/maintenance/SaaS subscriptions sold to consumers in other EU states** count toward the €10,000 threshold. Above it, foreign VAT is due, via OSS, which requires a VAT ID.
- **Non-EU clients (US, UK, CH):** B2B services fall outside the scope of Slovenian VAT.

**Crossing €60,000**
- After crossing €60,000, prices to consumers and non-VAT clients effectively rise by 22%.
- VAT-registered SME clients can deduct the VAT.
- From that point the s.p. could also deduct VAT on its own purchases, including the reverse-charge VAT.

### Gaps
- The VAT treatment of Stripe processing fees (possibly an exempt financial service) was not researched.
- The procedure and penalties for missing the Art. 79(1)(4) identification were not confirmed in an official FURS explanation.
- Whether the voluntary opt-in period in Art. 94(9) is really only the current and following year (it was 5 years under older law) should be confirmed.
- Whether bespoke web development sold to EU consumers is ever treated as an electronically supplied service was not confirmed.

## 4. Invoicing: davčno potrjevanje računov, Stripe card payments, invoice contents, the book of issued invoices, B2B e-invoicing from 2028 and payment terms

### Takeaway
- **Fiscalisation:** invoices paid by **bank transfer** need no fiscal verification. Invoices paid in "cash" in the ZDavPR sense must be confirmed by FURS before they are issued, and that definition **includes payment and credit cards**.
- **Stripe is not settled:** FURS's own Q&A (Q220) explicitly asks whether Stripe, Klarna and Sofort payments are cash or non-cash. The reported FURS position is that payments through a licensed **payment service provider** that pays out to the merchant's account are non-cash (as with PayPal). A Stripe-specific answer was not retrieved, so get written confirmation or fiscalise card-paid invoices to be safe.
- **E-invoicing:** structured B2B e-invoices become **mandatory from 1 January 2028** for every PRS entity, including s.p.
- **Payment terms:** B2B payment terms are capped at **60 days**, and late payers owe **€40** recovery compensation per invoice.

### Cited Findings
- **ZDavPR definition (gov.si, last updated 18 Jul 2025):** "Kot plačilo z gotovino se šteje plačilo z bankovci in kovanci, pa tudi plačilo s plačilno ali kreditno kartico" — cash payment includes banknotes, coins, **debit and credit cards**, cheques and similar methods, but **not direct transfers to a transaction account**.
  - Invoices paid in cash must be confirmed by FURS **before** issuance via an electronic device.
  - In certain cases a vezana knjiga računov (bound invoice book) or later submission is allowed.
  - From 1 Jan 2026, vending machines report in real time instead.

  Source: [gov.si: Davčni postopek in davčno potrjevanje računov](https://www.gov.si/teme/davcni-postopek-in-davcno-potrjevanje-racunov/)
- **FURS Q&A on online payments (as reproduced by racunovodstvo.net, dated 9 Feb 2016; answers not visible on that page):**
  - **Q220** (3 Dec 2015 / 9 Feb 2016) asks whether payment methods used in online sales — **Stripe, Klarna, Sofort banking, PayMill, Braintree** — are cash or non-cash, and "Kdaj se izvajalec spletnih plačil šteje kot ponudnik plačilnih storitev?" (when does an online payment processor count as a payment service provider?);
  - **Q307** covers card payments via PayMill/Braintree;
  - **Q186** asks whether PayPal is cash or non-cash.

  Source: [racunovodstvo.net: Davčno potrjevanje računov (FURS Q&A)](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/pojasnila/10356/davcno-potrjevanje-racunov)
- **Reported FURS positions:**
  - PayPal was treated as **non-cash**, and FURS later treated Moneta "the same as PayPal" — [data.si](https://data.si/blog/davcne-blagajne-enako-kot-paypal-se-bo-obravnavala-tudi-moneta/);
  - payments through PayPal-type payment service providers did not need confirmation, but **POS-terminal card payments were treated as cash** — [data.si: Davčne blagajne tudi pri prodaji na spletu](https://data.si/blog/davcne-blagajne-tudi-pri-prodaji-na-spletu/);
  - a 2020 blog quoting FURS says a payment received through an online payment provider is non-cash **if that provider has payment-service-provider status under ZPlaSS**, but if the provider only acts as the seller's agent the payment counts as cash — [Shopamine](https://www.shopamine.com/sl/support/blog/spletna-trgovina-in-davcne-blagajne). This is a secondary and older source.
- **B2B e-invoicing:**
  - The DZ adopted the *Zakon o izmenjavi elektronskih računov in drugih elektronskih dokumentov* (often abbreviated ZIERDED) on 23 Oct 2025.
  - **From 1 January 2028**, paper invoices between businesses are no longer allowed.
  - **Who is covered:** all entities in the Poslovni register **and sole proprietors**.
  - **Format:** a structured **XML** document; **a PDF is not an e-invoice**.
  - **Exchange:** via e-route (e-pot) providers, own systems, **PEPPOL**, or the **free miniBlagajna app** for low-volume entities.
  - **Email is not allowed**, except to consumers.
  - **Exceptions:** consumers (paper unless they consent to e-invoices) and foreign companies.
  - **No reporting** of e-invoices to FURS.

  Source: [gov.si, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-10-23-drzavni-zbor-sprejel-zakon-o-izmenjavi-elektronskih-racunov-in-drugih-elektronskih-dokumentov/); see also [PwC Slovenija](https://www.pwc.com/si/sl/aktualno/Zakon-o-izmenjavi-elektronskih-racunov-in-drugih-elektronskih-dokumentov.html). The provider-registration chapter reportedly applies earlier, from **1 April 2027** (secondary source) — [Lookuptax](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/slovenia/zierded-b2b-einvoicing-2028)
- **Invoice register:** the register of issued invoices must contain at least the sequential entry number and the invoice number and date, and is closed as of 31 December. This is from the Pravilnik o poslovnih knjigah in drugih davčnih evidencah, an older consolidated text — [racunovodstvo.net](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/predpis/1503/pravilnik-o-poslovnih-knjigah-in-drugih-davcnih-evidencah-za-osebe-ki-dosegajo-dohodek-z-opravljanjem-dejavnosti). Normiranci keep a **knjiga izdanih računov** among their ongoing duties — [data.si: Vodenje poslovnih knjig za normirance](https://data.si/vodenje-poslovnih-knjig-za-normirance/)
- **Non-VAT s.p. invoices** may not show VAT (ZDDV-1 Art. 94(8)) — [racunovodstvo.net](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zddv/94-clen)
- **ZPreZP-1 payment terms:**
  - the B2B payment term may not exceed **60 days** from receipt of the invoice;
  - longer terms only exceptionally, agreed **in writing** and not grossly unfair;
  - public authorities have **30 days**;
  - a creditor may claim **€40** recovery-cost compensation per late receivable.

  Source: [Uradni list RS 57/2012 (ZPreZP-1)](https://www.uradni-list.si/_pdf/2012/Ur/u2012057.pdf); [racunovodstvo.net: ZPreZP-1](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zprezp)

### Inferences
**Lowest-friction set-up**
- Invoice packages and subscriptions for **payment to the transaction account** (UPN/QR or SEPA, including SEPA direct debit for subscriptions). These need no fiscalisation.
- If Stripe card checkout is used, either:
  - obtain a written FURS answer (or the accountant's confirmation) that Stripe, as a licensed PSP paying out to your account, counts as non-cash; or
  - issue card-paid invoices from a Slovenian invoicing app that supports davčno potrjevanje (special digital certificate from eDavki).

**Fiscalisation is not limited to VAT-registered s.p.** It most likely also applies to a non-VAT-registered s.p., because ZDavPR follows the ZDDV-1 duty to issue invoices, which covers small taxpayers too. Confirm with the accountant.

**Invoice contents to include** (ZDDV-1 Art. 82 logic, not verified article by article here):
- sequential number and date of issue;
- supplier name, address, davčna številka and SI VAT ID (if any);
- customer name and address, plus the customer's VAT ID for EU B2B;
- description of the service and the supply date or period;
- price;
- the exemption note, commonly "DDV ni obračunan na podlagi 1. odstavka 94. člena ZDDV-1";
- for EU B2B clients, the reverse-charge note;
- payment terms and reference (sklic).

**Prepare now for 2028:** choose invoicing software that already outputs e-SLOG/PEPPOL e-invoices so the 2028 switch needs no rework.

**Late-payment clauses:** put zakonske zamudne obresti and the €40 compensation into the B2B general terms (splošni pogoji). Use a 30-day or shorter payment term with advance payments.

## 5. Contracts and terms: website-service contract and splošni pogoji (scope, IP and ZASP, liability, subscriptions, SLAs) and B2C rules under ZVPot-1

### Takeaway
Use a written contract plus splošni pogoji. Under **ZASP**:
- the author keeps all rights not expressly transferred;
- transferring a copy or ownership of the "object" does not transfer copyright;
- onward transfer needs the author's consent unless the contract allows it;
- fair remuneration rules cannot be waived.

So the contract must spell out **which rights**, exclusive or not, for which territory and duration, and **when** they pass (typically on full payment). It should also separate the client's bespoke deliverables from your reusable "factory" code and templates, which you **license**.

For consumers under **ZVPot-1**, give the 14-day withdrawal information and model form. Get **prior express consent** to start work within the withdrawal period, plus an **acknowledgment** that the right is lost once the service is fully performed. Otherwise the consumer can withdraw and owe nothing, or owe only a proportionate amount.

### Cited Findings
**ZASP (copyright)**
- Under **Art. 21** the author "izključno dovoljuje ali prepoveduje uporabo svojega dela" (exclusively permits or prohibits use of the work), and use is lawful only if the relevant material right has been transferred. Under **Art. 41**, transferring ownership of the object does not transfer the author's rights, and vice versa, unless law or contract says otherwise — [racunovodstvo.net: ZASP (public part)](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zasp)
- **Art. 81:**
  - authors are entitled to fair and proportionate remuneration when they transfer material rights;
  - if the remuneration is "očitno nesorazmerno nizko" (clearly disproportionately low) compared with later revenues, the author can demand an adjustment;
  - that right cannot be waived (para. 5).

  Version in force from 26 Oct 2022 — [racunovodstvo.net: ZASP 81. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zasp/81-clen)
- **Onward transfer:** a holder of material rights cannot transfer them to third parties without the author's permission unless the contract provides otherwise (**Art. 78(1)**). The Supreme Court (II Ips 513/2006) applied *in dubio pro auctore*. This is from search-surfaced case-law notes — [TFL](https://tax-fin-lex.si/dokument/podrobnosti/fc4685b8dca641cb89f195a594e2a73b)
- **What the contract should specify:** a Slovenian university publisher's guide says the transfer of material copyrights is governed by a **written** contract stating precisely the manner, scope, duration and territory of each use and the fee — [Založba UL guide](https://ebooks.uni-lj.si/ZalozbaUL/catalog/download/673/1049/10921?inline=1)

**ZVPot-1 (consumers)** — Uradni list RS, št. 130/2022, applicable from 25 Jan 2023
- **Art. 141(3):** if a consumer who asked for a service to start during the withdrawal period then withdraws, they pay "znesek v sorazmerju z opravljeno storitvijo do dneva obvestitve podjetja o odstopu" — an amount proportionate to the service performed up to notification. The share is computed on the total contract value, or on market value if the price is excessive — [racunovodstvo.net: ZVPot-1 141. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zvpot/141-clen)
- **Art. 141(4):** the consumer owes **nothing** if:
  - the company did not give the information required by Art. 130(1) points 6 and 9; or
  - the consumer did not make the express request to start under Art. 132(7) or 133(3).

  **Art. 141(5)** sets a similar rule for digital content: no cost if there was no prior consent, no acknowledgement of losing the right, or no confirmation — [racunovodstvo.net: ZVPot-1 141. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zvpot/141-clen)
- **Exceptions to the withdrawal right (Art. 135):** the consumer cannot withdraw from a paid service contract once the company has **fully performed** it, if performance began with the consumer's **prior express consent** and the consumer **acknowledged** losing the right on full performance. The **14-day withdrawal period** is in **Art. 134**. Both are from a search summary of the consolidated text — [racunovodstvo.net: ZVPot-1 135. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zvpot/135-clen)
- **Art. 143:** on withdrawal the company refunds all payments without delay and **within 14 days at the latest**, using the same payment method — [racunovodstvo.net: ZVPot-1 143. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zvpot/143-clen)
- **Art. 142(2):** the consumer is not liable for diminished value if not informed of the withdrawal right under Art. 130(1)(6) — [racunovodstvo.net: ZVPot-1 142. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zvpot/142-clen)
- **Model forms:** the official model withdrawal form and instructions are in the Pravilnik o obrazcih v zvezi z uveljavljanjem pravice do odstopa od pogodbe — [gov.si (MGTS)](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MGTS/Dokumenti/DNT/varstvo-potrosnikov/Predpisi-na-podlagi-ZVPot-1/Pravilnik-o-obrazcih-v-zvezi-z-uveljavljanjem-pravice-do-odstopa-od-pogodbe-sklenjene-na-daljavo-ali-zunaj-poslovnih-prostorov.pdf)

**Other contract-relevant rules**
- **B2B payment terms:** maximum 60 days and €40 compensation (ZPreZP-1) — [Uradni list RS 57/2012](https://www.uradni-list.si/_pdf/2012/Ur/u2012057.pdf)
- **Hosting plan:** Vercel's **Hobby plan is for non-commercial personal use only**, and "all commercial usage of the platform requires either a Pro or Enterprise plan" (guidelines updated 27 Feb 2026) — [Vercel Fair Use Guidelines](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines). Vercel's DPA covers **Pro and Enterprise** customers — [Vercel DPA](https://vercel.com/legal/dpa)

### Inferences
**Clause checklist for the pogodba o izdelavi spletne strani and splošni pogoji**

*Parties, scope and acceptance*
1. **Parties and ZEPT/ZVPot identification** of the s.p.
2. **Scope:** the package definition and the configurator output treated as a binding offer (ponudba), with what is *not* included (copywriting, photos, translations, SEO campaigns).
3. **Revisions:** for example two rounds per milestone, with extra work at an hourly rate.
4. **Client duties:** content and credentials delivered by date X. If late, deadlines move, a "deemed acceptance" or parking fee applies after N days, or the project closes with the deposit retained (B2B).
5. **Price and payment:** an advance (avans) of, say, 30–50% before work starts; balance on acceptance; for B2B, a payment term of 8–30 days (ZPreZP-1 cap 60), zakonske zamudne obresti, €40 compensation, and a right to suspend hosting or work for non-payment.
6. **Acceptance procedure:** test link, a review period of X days, then silence counts as acceptance (B2B).
7. **Warranty for defects (jamčevanje za napake):** what counts as a defect (deviation from the agreed spec), notification deadlines and the remedy (repair). Browser and device support matrix.

*Liability and IP*

8. **Limitation of liability:** a cap, for example fees paid in the last 12 months; exclusion of indirect damage and lost profit; client responsible for content legality (copyright, GDPR notices of its own business). Under general obligations law, liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be excluded (OZ; not verified here).
9. **IP and ZASP:**
   - an explicit list of transferred material rights (reproduction, distribution, making available to the public, adaptation/alteration, further transfer per Art. 78);
   - exclusive or non-exclusive, worldwide, for the full term of protection;
   - transfer **upon full payment**;
   - **pre-existing and factory components** (generator, templates, components, configurator logic) stay yours and are licensed non-exclusively and perpetually;
   - **open-source components** under their own licences (MIT, Apache-2.0 and so on), listed in the delivery;
   - **third-party assets** (fonts, stock photos, icons) under their licences, ideally bought in the client's name;
   - your right to show the work in a portfolio.
10. **Domain and accounts:** register the .si domain in the **client's name**; you act as technical contact. Hosting is either under your Vercel Pro team (a managed-service subscription) or transferred to the client's own Vercel/Supabase accounts on exit. Spell out an **exit or handover clause**: code export, database dump, DNS transfer.

*Subscriptions, SLA and other annexes*

11. **Subscription (hosting/maintenance):**
    - term (monthly or annual), auto-renewal, notice period, price indexation;
    - what "maintenance" covers (updates, backups, X minor content edits a month, security patches);
    - suspension for non-payment;
    - data return and deletion at the end.
12. **SLA:** an uptime target that is realistic given the dependence on Vercel/Supabase uptime; response and fix times by severity; maintenance windows; backup frequency and retention; exclusions (client changes, third-party outages, force majeure); service credits rather than damages.
13. **DPA annex** (see Q6) and **AI feature terms** (see Q9).
14. **Accessibility:** what conformance level you target, and that client-supplied content (alt texts, PDFs, videos) is the client's responsibility (see Q8).
15. **Governing law and court:** Slovenia, with the court at your seat (B2B only; for consumers, the consumer's protections apply).

**B2C checklist (ZVPot-1)**
- pre-contract information under Art. 130 (identity, total price including all costs, withdrawal right and form, duration and termination of subscriptions);
- confirmation of the contract on a durable medium;
- a **separate unticked checkbox:** "Izrecno soglašam, da začnete z izvedbo storitve pred iztekom 14-dnevnega odstopnega roka, in se zavedam, da izgubim pravico do odstopa, ko bo storitev v celoti opravljena" (I expressly agree that you start the service before the 14-day withdrawal period ends, and I understand I lose the right to withdraw once the service is fully performed);
- the model withdrawal form;
- refunds within 14 days.

For ongoing subscriptions, the right is never "fully performed", so withdrawal within 14 days means a proportionate refund.

## 6. GDPR: the s.p. as controller for its own inquiries, as processor for client websites, sub-processors, privacy policy, records, retention and 72-hour breach notification (and ZVOP-2 specifics)

### Takeaway
**For sites.jurelabs.si** (inquiries, client data) the s.p. is a **controller**. It needs:
- a privacy notice (Art. 13);
- a record of processing activities, because the processing is not occasional;
- a retention rule;
- breach procedures, including notification to the **Informacijski pooblaščenec within 72 hours**.

**For client websites, forms, databases and chatbots it hosts or maintains**, it is a **processor**. It must sign an **Art. 28 DPA** (pogodba o obdelavi osebnih podatkov) with each client and pass equivalent terms down to **sub-processors**:

## 7. Cookies and marketing: ZEKom-2 consent rules, cookieless analytics and cold email/B2B outreach

### Takeaway
**Cookies:** ZEKom-2 Art. 225 is a **prior-consent (opt-in)** rule. Anything stored on or read from the user's device needs consent unless it is strictly necessary for the service the user explicitly requested, or purely technical for transmission. Analytics and marketing cookies or pixels need a consent banner. Truly cookieless analytics that store or read nothing on the device fall outside Art. 225, but GDPR still applies to IP addresses and similar data.

**Cold email (ZEKom-2 Art. 226):**
- **Natural persons:** prior consent is required, apart from the existing-customer exception.
- **Legal persons:** the consent rule does not apply, but the sender must be identified and every message must carry a working opt-out address.
- **Named people:** ZEKom-2 Art. 226(6) allows using a person's email if their organisation published it as its contact address. ZVOP-2 Art. 93 forbids using public-source contact data for direct marketing. The safest route is generic company addresses of **legal persons** (d.o.o., d.d.), not s.p.-s. Get legal advice before scaling outreach.

### Cited Findings
**ZEKom-2 Art. 225 (cookies)** — version in force from 14 Feb 2023
- Storing or accessing information in the terminal equipment is allowed **only with consent**, after clear and complete information about the controller and purposes (per data-protection rules).
- **Exceptions:**
  - storage or access "izključno zaradi prenosa sporočila" (solely to transmit a communication);
  - storage or access strictly necessary for an information-society service "ki jo naročnik ali uporabnik izrecno zahtevata" (that the subscriber or user explicitly requested).
- Consent may be expressed via browser or application settings where technically feasible and compliant with data-protection law.
- Supervision: "Inšpekcijski nadzor nad izvajanjem določb tega člena opravlja Informacijski pooblaščenec" (the Information Commissioner carries out inspection).

Source: [racunovodstvo.net: ZEKom-2 225. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zekom/225-clen)

**ZEKom-2 Art. 226 (unsolicited communications)** — version in force from 14 Feb 2023
- **(1)** direct marketing by automated calling systems, SMS/MMS, fax or **email** requires the subscriber's or user's **prior consent**;
- **(2)** existing customers: a business that obtained an email address in a sale may market its own **similar** products or services, offering a free and easy opt-out at collection and in every message;
- **(3)** other electronic direct marketing also requires consent;
- **(4)** the consent rules of paras. 1 and 3 apply only where the subscriber is a **natural person**;
- **(5)** email that hides or disguises the sender's identity (contrary to ZEPT), or lacks a valid address to stop messages, is prohibited;
- **(6)** a legal (or natural) person may use an individual's email address if the individual's organisation **publicly published it as its contact address**;
- **(7)** anyone harmed may sue or seek an injunction.

Sources: [racunovodstvo.net: ZEKom-2 226. člen](https://www.racunovodstvo.net/zakonodaja/zekom/226-clen); [TFL: ZEKom-2 226. člen](https://tax-fin-lex.si/dokument/podrobnosti/50729148-0b15-4f1a-92c6-2dd305fa072e/clen/05cf1f63-52fd-4dbf-a09a-9e2b33e3f78f). The two summaries word para. 6 slightly differently.

**ZVOP-2 Art. 93(1):** public-source or voluntarily given contact data may be used for meetings and trainings "razen izvajanja neposrednega trženja" (except direct marketing) — [Uradni list RS: ZVOP-2](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2022-01-4187/zakon-o-varstvu-osebnih-podatkov-zvop-2)

**IP RS cookie guidance (weak source):** a news and forum source reports that the IP RS **removed** its older cookie "good practice" guidelines. Those guidelines had allowed some analytics (e.g., Matomo) without consent. This is unverified and older — [slo-tech.com](https://slo-tech.com/novice/povezano/35898)

### Inferences
**Consent banner needed on the user's own site and client sites for:**
- Google Analytics, Meta/LinkedIn pixels and Hotjar-type tools;
- embedded YouTube or Maps that set cookies;
- third-party chat widgets that set identifiers.

**No consent needed for:** session and auth cookies, a shopping cart, CSRF/security tokens, load-balancing cookies, and the consent-preference cookie itself.

Offer a **"cookieless by default"** stack as a selling point: self-hosted fonts, server-side or cookieless analytics (no localStorage, no fingerprinting), click-to-load embeds. Keep a privacy notice for the server-side processing.

**Legal basis for outreach:** use GDPR legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) with a documented balancing test. In the **first message**, give the Art. 14 information: where you got the address, purposes, rights, and an easy objection link. Honour objections permanently with a suppression list, since Art. 21(3) GDPR makes objection to direct marketing absolute.

**Target segmentation:** many targets "without websites" are **s.p.-s**, who are natural persons. ZEKom-2 Art. 226(1)/(4) likely require **prior consent** for marketing emails to them. Safer channels: personalised letters by post, phone (ZEKom-2 rules for voice were not researched), or contact forms where the business invites offers. Again, get legal confirmation.

### Gaps
- No current official IP RS guidance on cookies or analytics was retrieved.
- No official interpretation was found of whether an **s.p.** counts as a "natural person subscriber" under ZEKom-2 Art. 226(4).
- It is unclear how ZEKom-2 Art. 226(6) and ZVOP-2 Art. 93(1) interact for named employee emails.
- ZEKom-2 fines for Arts. 225 and 226, and the rules on cold phone calls, were not researched.

## 8. Accessibility: European Accessibility Act, Slovenian ZDPSI, micro-enterprise exemption, WCAG/EN 301 549, ZDSMA for public bodies, and selling accessibility as a feature

### Takeaway
Slovenia implemented the EAA through **ZDPSI** (Zakon o dostopnosti do proizvodov in storitev za invalide), adopted 24 Jan 2023 and **applicable from 28 June 2025**, amended by **ZDPSI-A** in February 2026.
- **In scope:** consumer-facing services such as **e-commerce**, consumer banking, e-books, passenger-transport websites, apps and e-tickets, and electronic communications.
- **Exempt:** **micro-enterprises providing services** (<10 persons and ≤€2m turnover or balance sheet).
- **Not in scope:** most of the user's SME clients with plain brochure sites, and small e-shops of micro-enterprises.
- **In scope:** larger clients' e-shops and booking or banking flows.

Public-sector websites fall under **ZDSMA** (accessibility statement, EN 301 549). Accessibility can be sold as a quality feature ("built to WCAG 2.2 AA, with an accessibility statement and audit report"), but legal compliance should not be guaranteed.

### Cited Findings
- **ZDPSI adoption and scope:** the Zakon o dostopnosti do proizvodov in storitev za invalide was adopted by the DZ on **24 January 2023** and applies from **28 June 2025**.
  - **Services covered:** audiovisual media services; electronic communications; parts of air, bus, rail and water passenger transport (websites, mobile services, e-tickets, information); consumer banking; e-books; **e-commerce**; 112 emergency calls.
  - **Products covered:** computers, OSs, terminals, phones, e-readers and others.

  Source: [gov.si, 27 Jan 2023](https://www.gov.si/novice/2023-01-27-z-novim-zakonom-do-vec-dostopnosti-proizvodov-in-storitev-za-invalide/). Gazette entry: [Uradni list RS 2023-01-0238](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2023-01-0238)
- **Micro-enterprise exemption:** the law **does not apply to micro-enterprises that provide services** (ZDPSI Art. 2, per search summary). The EU summary of the Directive says the same — [EUR-Lex summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/SL/legal-content/summary/accessibility-of-products-and-services.html). GZS (Oct 2024) lists e-commerce and digital services among covered items and discusses micro-enterprises — [GZS posvet](https://www.gzs.si/Portals/Panoga-PTZ/Vsebine/dogodki-galerije/sporocilo%20posvet%20PTZ%203%2010.2024.pdf)
- **Archived content:** website and app content that counts as an archive, i.e. not updated after 28 June 2025, is excluded (per search summary of the law).
- **ZDPSI-A** (Uradni list 2026, published Feb/March 2026):
  - e-commerce providers must provide information on the accessibility of products and services offered, where the responsible economic operator supplies it;
  - accessibility requirements were made mandatory conditions in public procurement.

  Source: [Uradni list RS 2026-01-0563 (ZDPSI-A)](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2026-01-0563/zakon-o-spremembah-in-dopolnitvah-zakona-o-dostopnosti-do-proizvodov-in-storitev-za-invalide-zdpsi-a); consolidated text dated 25 Feb 2026 on [TFL](https://www.tax-fin-lex.si/Dokument/Podrobnosti/08118d41-8bc8-4632-ba3c-9ed8371d7753)
- **EU definitions (Directive (EU) 2019/882):**
  - **micro-enterprise:** fewer than 10 persons and annual turnover ≤ €2m or balance sheet ≤ €2m;
  - **e-commerce services:** services provided at a distance through websites and mobile services, by electronic means, at the individual request of a consumer, with a view to concluding a consumer contract;
  - microenterprises providing services are exempt;
  - conformity with harmonised standards gives a presumption of conformity.

  Source: [EUR-Lex: Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)
- **ZDSMA (public sector):** Zakon o dostopnosti spletišč in mobilnih aplikacij, Ur. l. RS 30/18, amended 95/21, 189/21 and 18/23. Public bodies must publish an accessibility statement. Example statements cite SIST EN 301 549 compliance — [SID banka statement](https://www.sid.si/sites/www.sid.si/files/documents/izjava_o_dostopnosti_-_sid_final.pdf); [IRSSV statement, Feb 2026](https://irssv.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Izjava-o-dostopnosti_posodobljena_16.2.2026.pdf). The inspectorate reviewed **324** accessibility statements in its inspections — [gov.si, 12 Dec 2023](https://www.gov.si/novice/2023-12-12-predstavitev-najpogostejsih-napak-po-zakonu-o-dostopnosti-spletisc-in-mobilnih-aplikacij-zdsma/). Complaints go to the information society inspectorate — [SPOT: izjava o dostopnosti](https://spot.gov.si/sl/teme/izjava-o-dostopnosti)

### Inferences
**Which clients are in scope**
- **Out of scope:** a plain presentation site for a hairdresser, plumber or restaurant. It is not an "e-commerce service" because no consumer contract is concluded online. Online table or appointment booking for a micro-business is also out of scope through the micro exemption.
- **Probably in scope:** an online shop, paid booking or subscription sign-up run by a client with **≥10 staff or >€2m turnover**. The client is the obligated "service provider", and you are their supplier. The contract should say what you deliver (an accessible template and components) and what the client must do (content, alt texts, accessible PDFs, the accessibility information or statement).
- **Public bodies** (municipalities, schools, public institutes): ZDSMA requires EN 301 549 conformance (which includes WCAG 2.1 AA) and a compliant accessibility statement.

**How to sell it**
- Build every template to **WCAG 2.2 AA** as standard; it costs little in an automated factory.
- Run automated checks (axe/Lighthouse) plus a short manual checklist.
- Ship an "izjava o dostopnosti" generator.
- Offer a paid "accessibility audit and statement" add-on for clients in scope.

Avoid wording like "guaranteed EAA compliance". Use "designed and tested against WCAG 2.2 AA / EN 301 549 criteria".

### Gaps
- The ZDPSI article numbers, the supervising inspectorate(s) and the fine amounts were not retrieved.
- Whether a micro-enterprise selling **goods** online benefits from the services exemption is debated: the search summary says this is not settled by Slovenian sources.
- Whether EN 301 549 has been formally cited as a harmonised standard under the EAA by October 2026 was not verified.

## 9. EU AI Act: Article 50 transparency (chatbots, AI-generated content), application dates after the Digital Omnibus, and obligations when deploying chatbots on client sites

### Takeaway
**Article 50 transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026.** The AI "Digital Omnibus", **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** (published in the OJ on 24 July 2026), delayed the high-risk rules but **not** Article 50. The one exception is a short transition **to 2 December 2026** for the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking of AI-generated content (sources differ slightly on its scope).

For chatbots on client sites:
- the system must make clear that users are talking to an AI, unless obvious;
- if you build the chatbot under your own name, you are likely the **provider** of the AI system;
- the client operating it on its site is a **deployer**.

AI-written website copy normally needs no label unless it is published to inform the public on matters of public interest without human editorial review. Deepfakes always need disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- **Digital Omnibus timeline:**
  - Parliament adopted it on 16 June 2026 and the Council on 29 June 2026; published in the OJ on **24 July 2026** (OJ:L_202601744);
  - **Article 50 became applicable on 2 August 2026**;
  - Article 50(2) obligations (synthetic content) were postponed to **2 December 2026** for certain existing systems or GPAI models;
  - high-risk Annex III systems moved to **2 December 2027** (from 2 Aug 2026), Annex I systems to **2 August 2028**;
  - the **Article 4 AI literacy** duty was softened to "supporting the development" of AI literacy, with no guaranteed level.

  Source: [FPF, 28 Jul 2026, updated 5 Aug 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)
- **Regulation number and transition:** the Omnibus is **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, in force since 27 July 2026. Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026 "as originally planned". Generative systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026 for machine-readable marking; systems launched from 2 Aug 2026 had to comply from launch — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Praxikon](https://www.praxikon.com/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026). **Conflict:** one source frames the 2 Dec date as a GPAI-model postponement rather than a legacy-system grace period (per search summary).
- **Article 50 content (AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689):**
  - **50(1):** providers must design AI systems that interact directly with people so that people are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless obvious from the context;
  - **50(2):** providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must mark outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way;
  - **50(4):** deployers must disclose deepfakes, and must disclose AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest unless it underwent human review and someone holds editorial responsibility;
  - **50(5):** the information must be clear and distinguishable, at the latest at the first interaction or exposure.

  Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

### Inferences
**Chatbot product requirements**
- A visible "AI asistent" label and a first message such as "Sem AI pomočnik …" (I am an AI assistant). Keep it in the transcript UI.
- An escalation-to-human option.
- No impersonation of staff.
- If the bot generates images or audio for end users, use a model or API that applies machine-readable marking (C2PA/watermark) and keep that capability on.

**Contract allocation**
- In the chatbot add-on terms, state that you are the provider of the chatbot system and the client is the deployer.
- The client is responsible for its content, knowledge base, and its use for any high-risk purpose (e.g., credit or employment decisions), which you should prohibit.
- Include a GDPR DPA covering chat transcripts, with the LLM vendor as a sub-processor (an EU data region where available, and no training on client data).

**AI-generated website content**
- Typical SME marketing copy is not "public interest" information, so no Art. 50(4) label is legally required. Human review before publishing is good practice and supports the editorial-responsibility exemption anyway.
- Avoid AI-generated photos of "real" staff or customers (deepfake risk), or label them.

**AI literacy:** after the Omnibus, documented internal know-how is enough (e.g., a short guide on prompt and data hygiene).

### Gaps
- Slovenia's national AI Act implementation (designated market-surveillance authority, penalty provisions) was not researched.
- The exact scope of the Art. 50(2) transition (legacy systems vs GPAI) differs between sources and should be read from Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 itself.

### Takeaway

### Inferences
**ZEPT/ZVPot footer and "Podatki o podjetju" page for sites.jurelabs.si**
- full firma, e.g. "[Ime Priimek] s.p., [dejavnost]";
- address of seat, email, matična številka, davčna številka / ID za DDV (if any), main activity K62.100;
- supervisory bodies where relevant (TIRS for consumer protection; the IP RS for data protection).

Every client site the factory generates should get the same auto-filled block. This makes "ZEPT-compliant legal pages" a sellable feature, together with an auto-generated privacy policy and cookie notice.

**Insurance**
- Ask Triglav, Generali, Sava and Grawe for **poklicna odgovornost (čiste premoženjske škode) + cyber**, sized to the liability cap in your splošni pogoji.
- Many SME contracts will not require it, but it backs the SLA and data-processing exposure.

**Subcontractors**
- Allowed as ordinary B2B contracts with other s.p./d.o.o.
- Make sure each subcontractor (a) **assigns copyright to you in writing** (ZASP; see Q5) so you can pass rights on to clients, and (b) signs a **sub-processing DPA** if they touch personal data.

**Retention:** keep invoices, contracts, DPAs, consent logs and email opt-out lists. Tax documents are commonly kept for 10 years (to be confirmed).
